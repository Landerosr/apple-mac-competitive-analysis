# MacBook Pro competitive research | 2021–2023

## Research scope and evidence status

Rodolfo Landeros compared MacBook Pro products with competitors available during 2021–2023, focusing on the Intel-to-Apple Silicon hardware transition, performance improvements and potential financial and equity-performance implications. This scope reflects the author's clarification. The historical facts below were verified for this presentation update; they should not be represented as a newly completed valuation study or as proof that all these sources were used in the original work.

The repository does not contain the original comparison notes, a complete list of competitor configurations, controlled benchmark observations or a reproducible stock-return analysis for 2021–2023. Consequently, it cannot substantiate a specific performance advantage over a named competitor, a measured stock correlation or a causal stock-price effect. The current product tables cover later products and cannot substitute for contemporaneous evidence.

## Financial context, not product-level attribution

Apple's [FY2023 Form 10-K, Products and Services Performance](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/320193/000032019323000106/aapl-20230930.htm) reports Mac net sales of **$35.190 billion in FY2021, $40.177 billion in FY2022 and $29.357 billion in FY2023**. Apple reports a 14% increase in FY2022 and a 27% decrease in FY2023; it attributes the latter primarily to lower laptop sales.

These are fiscal-year totals for the entire Mac category, not MacBook Pro sales or Apple Silicon incremental revenue. They show that stronger product capabilities did not coincide with uninterrupted category growth. Product announcements use calendar dates; fiscal-year financial results must be aligned before comparison.

## Investment thesis and limits

The research considered whether better performance and power efficiency could strengthen MacBook Pro's competitive position and support demand, premium pricing and investor expectations. These are potential mechanisms, not quantified outcomes. Costs, product mix, competition and customer adoption would also affect financial results.

A relationship between product releases and Apple's equity performance remains a hypothesis. This project does not report a correlation coefficient, event-study return or estimated causal contribution to AAPL returns. Apple's other businesses, interest rates, broader market conditions and changing earnings expectations are competing explanations; contemporaneous movement alone cannot identify the effect of a chip transition.

No DCF, comparable-company valuation, target share price or original-period revenue forecast is presented. The existing later forecasting and weighted-score tools are separate portfolio extensions, not valuation evidence for this research.

## Defensible project description

“Compared MacBook Pro products with competing laptops during Apple's 2021–2023 transition from Intel processors to Apple Silicon, evaluating performance improvements, competitive positioning and potential financial and equity-performance implications.”

This describes the author's research scope without asserting independently verified benchmark results, investment returns or valuation work. A stronger quantified claim would require the original comparison data and a reproducible method.
