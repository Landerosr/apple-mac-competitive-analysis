Original research · 2021–2023

MacBook Pro through the Apple Silicon transition.

I compared MacBook Pro products with competing laptops available during 2021–2023, considering performance improvements, competitive positioning and potential financial and equity-performance implications.

Product thesis

Better performance and power efficiency could support demand and investor expectations. Apple announced the transition in 2020, followed by M1 Pro / Max MacBook Pros in 2021 and M2 Pro / Max models in January 2023. Manufacturer performance claims are not independent benchmark results.

Financial context

Mac net sales were $35.190B in FY2021, $40.177B in FY2022 and $29.357B in FY2023. These category totals do not isolate MacBook Pro or the impact of Apple Silicon. Product improvements did not coincide with uninterrupted sales growth.

Evidence limits: The original competitor configurations, benchmark observations and stock-return dataset are not included here. The equity relationship is a thesis, not a measured correlation or proof that Apple Silicon caused stock gains. This research does not claim a DCF, comparable-company valuation or revenue forecast.

What would make waiting worthwhile?

A laptop that still does its job, an over-budget quote, or an unresolved software requirement are concrete reasons to wait. Product age alone is not a release forecast. We do not have a verified future release date or a price-history dataset, so we cannot claim a new Mac or a better deal is imminent.

Rules run in order: validate inputs → check software → check budget → assess replacement need → check workload fit → verify the quote. These rules do not estimate performance, resale value or the probability of a price drop. Published model prices are not matched configurations.
